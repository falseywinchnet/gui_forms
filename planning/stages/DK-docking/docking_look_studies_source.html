Docking look studies

The sixteen taste questions from the docking study (T1 to T8) and the PageView study (PT1 to PT8), each drawn in the default theme: Pearl Ribbon moved toward Plan Paint's royale blue. Pick one option per question, either with an option's ID chip or in the answer sheet at the end, and copy the line back.

Already decided, and drawn here as given

  • Postage-stamp drag (D1). At the start of a drag the panel disappears and its place closes up at once. A miniature animates from the panel's rectangle down to a stamp about 100 units long in about 220 ms and follows the pointer. At the drop it grows into the target; on Escape it grows back into its old place.
  • Position-sensitive targets (D2, D3). A cross of five tiles (top, left, tab into, right, bottom) appears only over the pane under the pointer. Each tile draws the resulting shape in blue on black and the selected one is lit. The window's outer edges are always global targets. No drop straight into auto-hide.
  • Layout (C1, C2, C3). Binary splits. One document area, always present. A tool panel tabs into documents only when its descriptor allows it, so the document area's centre tile is off in these demos.
  • Pins (E4). Only root-level items carry a pin: a lone panel or a group. Tabbing one panel into another makes a group with a fresh, not-auto-hidden pin.
  • Auto-hide (E1 to E3). A side tab's flyout opens on hover after a delay or on click, as an in-window overlay. An outside click closes it and passes through.
  • Floating (B5). Floating tool panels belong to the main window, stay above it only, move with it, and hide while the application is inactive.
  • Document tabs (H4, P13, P16, P22, P28). Tabs shrink, then overflow into a list of every document. A page chosen from the list takes the last visible position. Close buttons on the selected and hot tabs, with space reserved on every closable tab. No "+" button. Reordering slides live.
  • Vocabulary (J1 to J4). A pin mark; one dock-shape mark for the tiles; modified is a custom item state the theme shows; side-tab labels may be vertical.
  • Navigation pane (P30, P31, P34). Large buttons, a grip, an overflow icon strip and a configure menu; collapsed, a narrow bar with a vertical caption that opens the content in a popup.

The mock windows always show the light default theme, even when this page is dark. They are browser sketches of construction and colour, not the engine's pixels. The demos use T1-1, T4-1 and T7-1 as their base so they read as one family with File Manager; change those answers and the base changes with them.

Docking

T1 · docking study §8

Which docking look to start from

Where the canonical docking systems differ, which family should docked panels start from? The drag and the targets behave the same in all three.

Decided. The ribbon follows Office 2010 and earlier (RB-10). Panes are flat, never raised. The study leans to the VS 2005 to 2010 period for the same reason.

  • T1-1 House composite: Studio 2003 caption strips with a tiny identity key on the graphite chassis, as File Manager's panes (DNA-P04, DNA-P02), and Plan Paint's tab stack for documents.
  • T1-2 VS 2005 period in house colours: a pale blue-grey chassis, a blue gradient caption on the active tool panel, slanted document tabs.
  • T1-3 VS 2010 period in house colours: a navy chassis, gold for the active caption and the selected document. The dark structure is close to anti-model V101.
T2 · docking study §8, D2 settled

The look of the indicator tiles

How should the decided tiles be drawn, and how quiet should they be? The owner rejected theatrical drag overlays for file drag and drop (DDV-007-06), so quietness is part of the question.

Decided. Five tiles in a cross, shown only over the pane under the pointer, each a black tile with the resulting shape in blue, the selected one lit; edge tiles for the window's outer edges (one dock-shape mark, J2).

  • T2-1 Five separate tiles, no backplate.
  • T2-2 Five tiles joined on one cross-shaped backplate.
  • T2-3 Five tiles on a diamond backplate (the VS 2005 arrangement).
  • T2-4 Quiet: smaller tiles at reduced strength that fade in over 100 ms; only the lit tile is at full strength.

The samples keep the translucent area highlight (the working assumption) so only the tiles differ. Tiles sit above the stamp so it never hides them.

Live: drag a panel by its caption, or one panel by its tab

Escape puts the panel back. Ctrl held suppresses docking (D6-1).

T3 · docking study §8, D2 working assumption

The area highlight

Position selects the target and the lit tile shows it. Should the area the panel would take also be marked?

Decided. The tiles and the lit tile. The area highlight is a working assumption until this answer.

  • T3-1 A translucent blue tint with a 1-unit edge.
  • T3-2 A hatched outline with no fill, like the classic drag rectangle.
  • T3-3 None; the lit tile alone shows the result.

The samples use T2-1 tiles and the "bottom" target.

Live: move a panel slowly across the panes

The edge bands of a pane split it; its centre tabs into it.

T4 · docking study §8

Captions and tool tabs

How does the active panel's caption differ from an inactive one, and how plain are the tool tabs at the bottom of a stack?

Decided. Captions are a Studio 2003 strip with a tiny identity key (DNA-P04) carrying position, pin and close. The pin sits only on root-level items. Tool tabs appear at the bottom when a stack holds two or more panels. The document tab shape is PT1.

  • T4-1 Quiet: the identity key turns blue and the label darkens; bottom tabs are flat with a near-white plate for the selected one.
  • T4-2 Filled: the active caption is a royale-blue gradient with white text (VS's coloured caption); bottom tabs as T4-1.
  • T4-3 Tinted: a pale blue caption with a 2-unit accent line on top; bottom tabs are a small Plan Paint tab stack hanging below the content.
T5 · docking study §8, E1 to E4 settled

The auto-hide flyout

Which shadow does the flyout cast, and does it slide out or simply appear?

Decided. Side tabs on the window edge; vertical labels are allowed (J4) and their reading direction is PT8. Hover for 400 ms or a click opens; leaving for 500 ms closes an unfocused flyout. The flyout is an in-window overlay; an outside click closes it and passes through. The pin auto-hides the whole group.

  • T5-1 Slides out over 180 ms (smoothstep), with the RB-37 overlay shadow.
  • T5-2 Appears at once, with the RB-37 overlay shadow.
  • T5-3 Slides out, with the lighter E3 floating shadow.
  • T5-4 Appears at once, with the E3 shadow.

Frames are 0, 60, 120 and 180 ms after the trigger.

Live: rest on a side tab, or click it; the pin docks the group back

Explorer and Outline are auto-hidden as one group. This sketch has a left strip only.

T6 · docking study §8, with J7

Floating window chrome

Does a floating window use the host's title bar, or a caption GUI.Forms draws?

Decided. Floating windows are owned by the main window (no taskbar button), stay above it only and move with it. The framework moves them by its own capture drag on the caption (B7-1). One thin navy window keyline (DNA-F04) and the E5 window shadow either way. The float, dock and maximize marks are decided with this question (J7); the marks drawn here are proposals.

  • T6-1 The host's title bar in its smallest utility style; each stack keeps its own caption below it.
  • T6-2 A drawn caption. With one stack, the stack's caption is the window's caption. With two, a thin drawn strip carries the window's marks above the stacks.
  • T6-3 Drawn with one stack, the host's title bar with two (VS's rule).

The host bars are a neutral stand-in; each host draws its own.

T7 · docking study §8

Seams between docked panes

What separates two docked panes?

Decided. A seam is 3 units or thinner with a wider hit target (§7.2). A collapse grip is flat at rest and grows with pointer proximity (DDV-007-04). File Manager's panes share their seams and captions with docking (K1-1).

  • T7-1 File Manager's graphite seam, 3 units, with a grip of dots.
  • T7-2 Studio 2003's flat gap: near-white between keylined panes, no grip.
  • T7-3 A 1-unit blue-grey keyline; the grip shows on proximity.

Hover a seam to see its grip.

T8 · docking study §8, DDV-007-01

Drag proximity

When a panel is dragged from a floating window over the main window, how does the main window show drag_proximate?

Decided. A window under a drag regains destination emphasis without taking focus. While a floating tool window is key the main window shows secondary_participating and never looks disabled.

  • T8-1 The main window's caption returns to its key look while the stamp is over it.
  • T8-2 A 2-unit focus-blue ring around the window; its caption stays as it was.
  • T8-3 Only the pane under the pointer regains emphasis: its identity key lights.
  • T8-4 Nothing beyond the tiles.
Live: drag the Properties tab out of the floating window onto the main window

The floating window is key, so the main window is secondary. A drop where nothing accepts floats the panel (D4-1).

PageView

PT1 · PageView study §7

Tab shape

Which shape do strip tabs and document tabs take, and do they share it?

Decided. One PageStrip draws every presentation (P1-1). Plan Paint's tab stack is the ribbon's; a tab is not a button, so no tab gets a bulge.

  • PT1-1 Plan Paint's tab stack everywhere.
  • PT1-2 A quieter Studio 2003 strip everywhere.
  • PT1-3 Classic tabs joined to the page (today's TabControl seam), in house colours.
  • PT1-4 Plan Paint's stack for documents, Studio 2003 for strip tabs.

Each strip: selected, hot, pressed, disabled, and a tab with an icon; on top, then on the bottom.

PT2 · PageView study §7

How the selected tab joins its page

Drawn on Plan Paint's stack so only the join differs. With PT1-2 or PT1-3 the same three joins apply to that shape.

  • PT2-1 Open seam: the tab runs into the page with no edge (Plan Paint).
  • PT2-2 Accent underline: a royale-blue bar under the selected label; the page keeps its edge.
  • PT2-3 Plate: the selected tab is a closed plate on the rail. It reads close to a button, which the "a tab is not a button" lesson warns against.
PT3 · PageView study §7

Close and pin glyphs

Does the close glyph get a hover plate, and on a pinned tab does the pin sit beside close or replace it?

Decided. Close at the trailing end on the selected and hot tabs, with space reserved on every closable tab (P16-1). Pinned tabs are an opt-in capability and come first (H3-2, P24-1). The pin mark is J1.

  • PT3-1 A plate under the close glyph while the glyph itself is hot; pin beside close.
  • PT3-2 A hover plate; on pinned tabs the pin replaces close.
  • PT3-3 Bare: the glyph darkens on hover; pin beside close.
  • PT3-4 Bare; the pin replaces close.
PT4 · PageView study §7

Modified

How does the theme show a page in the modified state?

Decided. modified is a custom item state shown by the theme, with no new mark (J3). In each sample main.cpp (selected), view.hpp (hot) and view.cpp are modified; app.cpp is not.

  • PT4-1 A large dot in the close slot; close replaces it while the tab is hot.
  • PT4-2 An asterisk after the label.
  • PT4-3 An italic label.
  • PT4-4 A coral tint: a short coral bar under the label.
PT5 · PageView study §7

Pinned tabs

How do pinned tabs look in their group at the front? theme.hpp and main.cpp are pinned in each sample.

Decided. Pinned tabs are opt-in, first, and move only within their group (H3-2, P24-1). No preview tab.

  • PT5-1 Full width with a pin glyph.
  • PT5-2 Compressed to the icon only. Close to the "icon-only strips" anti-model (M064).
  • PT5-3 Full width, no glyph at rest, a small gap after the group; the pin shows on hover.
PT6 · PageView study §7

Overflow button

Which button opens the list of documents when the strip runs out of room?

Decided. Document tabs shrink, then overflow into a list of every document (H4-3). A page chosen from the list takes the last visible position, so the order at the front is kept (P13). The strip preset scrolls, with a list button (P11-1).

  • PT6-1 The overflow double chevron (RB-20, as on ToolStrip).
  • PT6-2 A down arrow.
  • PT6-3 The count of hidden pages and a down arrow.

In every list hidden pages are italic (as VS) and the selected page is bold; answer "other" with a note if you want no italic.

PT7 · PageView study §7

The Outlook-style navigation pane

Which material do the large buttons take, and what does the grip look like?

Decided. Header, content, grip, large buttons and an overflow icon strip; a configure menu (Show more, Show fewer, hidden pages) with an options event and no built-in dialog. Collapsed, a narrow bar with a vertical caption that opens the content in a popup (P30-1, P31-1). The buttons are tabs in a tab list (P34-1).

  • PT7-1 Office 2007 to 2010 banded buttons; the selected page in gold bands; a graphite grip with dots.
  • PT7-2 Flat Studio 2003 rows; the selected page in the blue selection; an etched grip.
  • PT7-3 Rows in Plan Paint's tab material, stacked; the selected page near-white; a rail-blue grip.

Each sample shows the expanded pane and the collapsed bar. The last figure shows the decided menu and popup. The collapsed caption's reading direction is PT8.

PT8 · PageView study §7, J4

Side tab text

On the left edge, which way do side-tab labels read?

Decided. Side tabs may have vertical labels (J4); the collapsed navigation pane's caption is vertical (P31-1). Each sample shows Explorer open, Search hot.

  • PT8-1 Rotated, reading bottom to top (VS on the left edge).
  • PT8-2 Rotated, reading top to bottom.
  • PT8-3 Horizontal text in a wider column: the icon above a short label. The collapsed pane keeps a vertical caption; add a note for its direction.
