.. cpp:struct:: AmbientEffect final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/theme_rules/theme_rules.hpp>``

   An ambient effect (E7): its part repaints as time passes, at most ``frames_per_second``, and reads time_in_state within one ``period`` (it wraps), so that the effect cycles. Its region is the part's extent. It runs only while a primitive draws the part and the part has a record, and stops in an occluded or minimized window, under reduced motion and while the application's motion switch is off (PresentationSettings::ambient_motion); the part then rests at ``pose``, the time within the period it shows when still, such as an activity object's static pose.

.. cpp:struct:: CachedPartRaster final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/part_table/engine_part_table.hpp>``

   A part's cached raster and what drew it: the primitive, every parameter (animated ones included, since they change the pixels), the target with its states restricted to those the primitive reads, and the extent. The record owns it; ``raster`` is the cache's reference, and recorded commands that composite the raster hold others. A material the target lent is kept as a copy, compared by value: the theme's recipe that lent it may be replaced, and another recipe may later occupy its address. The kept target's borrowed pointers are cleared. When the material has an image fill, ``image_revision`` is the image registry's revision it was drawn at, so any change to the registry redraws it.

.. cpp:class:: CompiledThemeRules final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/theme_rules/theme_rules.hpp>``

   A sheet compiled against a registry: the rules that survived, each declaration checked, in the order the cascade visits them. Immutable once made; a ThemeEngine owns one. Compiling never fails as a whole: an invalid declaration or rule is dropped with a diagnostic and the rest stands, so a broken rule cannot take the theme's other rules down with it.

.. cpp:class:: MotionClock final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/motion_clock/motion_clock.hpp>``

   One window's motion clock (T2 plan, MotionClock; GIVEN D1-1). The window owns it; it observes the window. It holds at most one frame request, armed only while some value it holds has not settled, however many values move; the request is disconnected once the last one settles, so a window at rest schedules no frames. Execution: every member runs on the window's UI thread. Frames, settling and the listeners' damage all happen inside the window's frame poll. Event order on a frame: each value enlisted before the frame starts is advanced to the frame's time, in the order of its record's slot (the same for the same sequence of starts and releases); a value that settles is released before its listener runs; then the owner is invalidated as the value declared, then its listener runs. Values started by a listener are first advanced on the next frame. A listener that throws is counted as a frame callback fault; its value jumps to its end. Ending motion (all by name): - finish_all: reduced motion turned on (GIVEN D2-1) or the window occluded or minimized (GIVEN D4-1). Every value jumps to its end and its listener runs once, with the cause. While either holds, a new move jumps to its end at once and nothing is scheduled. - release_owner: a control left the window. Its values jump to their ends without listeners. - cancel: the window closes (or cancels its frame requests). Every value jumps to its end without listeners, and the request is disconnected. A record is named by its handle; a stale handle (its record released) is rejected, so a value whose owner has gone is never advanced. Ambient effects (T2 plan, phase E7; design language §9.4) share the same request. The engine enlists a part with an ambient effect while it draws it; on each frame at least the effect's frame interval after its last, the part's extent repaints. An effect whose part was not repainted since its last frame (the part is no longer shown) is dropped, so an effect can never keep a window painting by itself. finish_all and cancel drop every effect too; finish_all repaints each part once, at its static pose.

.. cpp:class:: MotionValue final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/motion_value/motion_value.hpp>``

   One value. Ownership: a control owns it as a member (or through something the control owns), and names itself as the owner, so the owner outlives it. It neither copies nor moves, so the clock's record of it stays valid; it releases that record when it is destroyed. All of it runs on the UI thread of the owner's window. The settled conditions, in device pixels at the window's scale (extent times scale converts value units): - timed: the duration has elapsed; - follow and decay: the remaining gap is within motion_settle_tolerance; - spring: the gap is within it, and so is the movement over one 60 Hz frame at the current velocity. On settling the value snaps exactly to its target, so the last frame is the static rendering. Every move starts from where the value stands at that moment, with its velocity: a retarget mid-flight never jumps (design language §9.1).

.. cpp:struct:: PaintGroup final

   A paint group (T2 plan, E7; owner, 2026-10-21: "there are other things that group layer may be needed for"): commands drawn as one image, then composited once. - ``bounds``, in the current coordinates when the group begins, limits the group. .. cpp:struct:: PresentationState final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/theme_rules/theme_rules.hpp>``

   One state of a part's presentational state machine (E7; design language §9.6). The machine is in the first of its states whose ``when`` states the part's published states all include (an empty ``when`` always matches), and moves its property to ``target``. A part whose states match none rests at the property's own value: its cascade value, or 1 for opacity. When the machine enters this state from another, or the part first appears in it, ``enter_from``, if set, stands the property at that value first; this is how a part fades in when it appears (the docking tiles, T2 GIVEN C2-1). Otherwise every move starts from the value shown, so a transition retargets and never restarts. ``stops`` (E9.6, multi-stop @keyframes), at most maximum_motion_stops, make the move into this state pass each stop's value at its offset of the duration, offsets strictly increasing within (0, 1), values within the property's range; they need a timed curve. They play when the move into the state begins: on entering it from another state, or on appearing in it from ``enter_from``. A part that appears in the state without an entry value stands at its target, as before. An animated parameter shows each stop at its nearest keyframe level, as any moving value (E7r). The machine is presentational only: it reads the published states and writes nothing back, so it can neither create an observable state nor delay behaviour.

.. cpp:class:: ThemeEngine final

   ``#include <gui_forms/engine/theme_engine/theme_engine.hpp>``

   The theme engine's entry point (T2 plan, ThemeEngine). paint_part hands it every part; controls never call it directly. Ownership: a Theme owns its engine, and every window showing the theme shares it. An application's windows share one theme (ApplicationResources), so they share one engine. The engine is immutable once its theme is made: it holds the theme's compiled rules (the cascade, E4), below them the engine's own built-in rules (built_in_theme_rules, E7), and borrows the built-in primitive registry. What changes from frame to frame belongs to each window's EnginePartTable and MotionClock. The engine draws a part with a primitive only when the cascade selects a registered primitive for the part, its parameters validate, the cost is within budget (GIVEN F1-1) and the presentation is not forced high contrast. In every other case the built-in recipe draws it: the body paint_part had before the engine existed. A theme with no rules of its own draws every part with the built-in recipe except those the built-in rules address: the ribbon's command faces and group buttons, which take the dithered bulge and its rise, and the docking tiles, which fade in. A keyed part's transitions run on its window's motion clock (E7): the part's states select each machine's state, the property moves toward it, and the part repaints within its extent as it moves. Its opacity multiplies a primitive's composite in the shared blend; a part the built-in recipe draws is drawn as one paint group (Painter::begin_group) composited once at that opacity. A part painted without a key stands at its machines' targets and its ambient effect's pose.


   .. rubric:: Construction

   .. cpp:function:: ThemeEngine()

      An engine with only the built-in rules.

   .. cpp:function:: explicit ThemeEngine(const ThemeRuleSheet& sheet)

      An engine with ``sheet`` compiled against the built-in registry. Invalid rules and declarations are dropped with diagnostics; nothing throws for them.


   .. rubric:: Properties

   .. cpp:function:: const PrimitiveRegistry& registry() const noexcept

      The primitives the engine's rules may select.

   .. cpp:function:: const CompiledThemeRules& rules() const noexcept


   .. rubric:: Methods

   .. cpp:function:: PartPlan plan(const Control& control, const PartPaint& part) const noexcept

      The cascade's decision for one part of ``control``, without painting.

   .. cpp:function:: void paint(Painter& painter, const Control& control, const PartPaint& part) const

      Paints one part of ``control`` (see paint_part). A part with a key (PartPaint::key) and paintable bounds, painted by a control attached to a window, has its record in that window's part table made or updated first; a primitive then draws into the record's cached raster. A part without a key leaves no record; a primitive draws it into a raster of its own for this paint. If the primitive refuses the part, the built-in recipe draws it. A part with a blend (blend_property) is drawn inside a paint group of that composite.

   .. cpp:function:: PartHitRegion hit_region(const Control& control, const PartPaint& part) const noexcept

      The hit region of one part of ``control`` (E8, T1's hand-off): the part's bounds and the shape its primitive draws there, as plan() decides it. A part the built-in recipe draws, for any reason, has a rectangle. Themes choose a primitive's shape; they never set a hit region (design language §7.4 rule 1). Test a point with part_hit.

   .. cpp:function:: Rect text_slot(const Control& control, const PartPaint& part) const noexcept

      Where one part of ``control`` lays out its text (E8, T1's hand-off): the part's bounds less its primitive's content insets at the window's scale and text scale, as plan() decides it. Where the insets leave no room across or down, the slot shrinks to the bounds' centre line on that axis, so it never leaves the part. A part the built-in recipe draws has its bounds. Like the hit region, it ignores the presentational scale.

.. cpp:function:: void paint_part(Painter& painter, const Control& control, const PartPaint& part)

   Paints one part of ``control`` by its role (see PartPaint), through the engine of the control's effective theme. Roles that draw nothing of their own (region, edge, perimeter, sheen) are no-ops: the material of the surface they belong to draws them. An empty or non-finite ``bounds`` paints nothing. What disabled means (owner, 2026-10-20: "a change in font color or control highlighting. greyed out"): a disabled part greys the control's chrome, through the recipes' disabled states: the text colour of labels and captions, and the highlighting (edge, well, hot and selected plates). Content never changes: - the image role (T2 GIVEN H1-1) is a content picture, such as the docking stamp's snapshot, drawn exactly as given into ``bounds``, never tinted, dimmed or resized by a theme, disabled or not; - a content colour (GIVEN E2-1) is drawn exactly as given, disabled or not. An icon is chrome, the theme's or the command's symbol, and keeps its disabled treatment: 45 % opacity. The shadow role paints the theme's overlay shadow (owner decision RB-37): ``bounds`` is the rect of the surface that casts it (a tooltip's face, a menu's panel), with the corner radius of ``corner_radius`` or else of the ``family`` recipe, and the shadow falls outside it, down and right. Paint it before the surface. Only surfaces that really overlap content cast it; a control's own contact shadow stays in its material. Nothing is painted where the theme casts no overlay shadow (high contrast, by default). A control that paints a shadow part reports overlay_shadow_outsets from its visual_outsets, so that damage and replay cover the blur.

   How far ``control``'s theme's overlay shadow reaches outside the casting surface's rect, in the control's presentation (ordinary or high contrast). Zero on every side when the theme casts none.
